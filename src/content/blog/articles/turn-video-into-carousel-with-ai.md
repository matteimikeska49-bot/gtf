# How to Turn a Video into a Carousel with AI

You spent hours researching, scripting, filming, and editing a 60-second Reel or TikTok. It performs well, gets a few thousand views, and then disappears into the algorithmic abyss.

If you are only posting that idea once, you are leaving engagement on the table.

Short-form videos and text-based carousels appeal to two entirely different user behaviors. Videos capture the fast-scrolling, passive audience. Carousels capture the intentional, reading-focused audience who wants to save actionable advice. By turning your video scripts into carousels, you double your content output with zero extra ideation.

## The Difference Between Video and Slide Pacing

Converting a short-form video is much easier than repurposing a 2-hour podcast, but it still requires adaptation. You cannot simply copy and paste a script and expect it to read well.

:::cards
type: takeaways

### 1. The Hook Adaptation
In a video, your hook might be a dramatic pause or a visual transition. In a carousel, the hook must be purely typographical and instantly readable.

### 2. The Delivery
Video relies on your tone of voice and energy. A carousel relies on formatting (bold text, bullet points, negative space) to emphasize importance.

### 3. The Depth
You speak at about 150 words per minute. A carousel allows you to add deeper context, a bonus tip, or a mini-framework that you had to cut from the video for time.
:::

## Common Conversion Mistakes

:::cards
type: mistakes

### Overcrowding Slides
Just because you said a paragraph in 10 seconds doesn't mean it belongs on one slide. If a thought is long, split it across two slides.

### Ignoring the Visuals
A video has movement; a text carousel is static. You must use typography (large headers, distinct colors) to create visual interest.

### Missing the CTA Shift
In a video, you might point down and say "Link in bio." In a carousel, the CTA must be a dedicated final slide with clear text instructions.
:::
