# AI Carousel Workflow: From Idea to Ready-to-Publish Slides

Creating high-performing carousels for LinkedIn and Instagram usually involves a frustrating amount of friction. You have to write the text, count the characters, switch to design software, align text boxes, adjust font sizes, and export the files.

This manual process is the reason most creators are inconsistent. 

A modern AI carousel workflow changes the equation. It combines source analysis, writing, and design without replacing human review. Here is the step-by-step workflow from a raw idea to a checked carousel export, followed by publishing on your chosen platform.

## Step 1: Add the Source

When you have no clear angle yet, use the [topic-brainstorming prompts](/blog/how-to-brainstorm-carousel-topics-with-ai) to narrow the subject before submitting it for generation.

AI cannot invent your personal experience. The workflow starts with you. 

When you have an idea—a lesson learned, a client case study, a framework, a Reels link, a video, an audio note, a PDF, or screenshots—add it as the source. Do not worry about perfect slide structure yet.

The goal here is purely to capture the "meat" of your expertise.

## Step 2: Source Analysis and Structuring (AI Task)

Raw source material is hard to read. The workflow needs to find the core idea, remove the noise, and turn the message into a sequence.

GoToFlow can analyze the input, extract the main point, suggest a strong hook, and organize the carousel flow.
For a text-first source, the [guide to turning text into an AI carousel](/blog/text-to-carousel-ai) explains how to select and divide the material before layout.

:::prompts
### The Structure Prompt
```text
Here is my source material on [Topic].
Turn it into a clear carousel angle with a strong hook,
logical slide flow, and one CTA.
```
:::

Review the output. Ensure the AI didn't remove any of your unique tone of voice or crucial context. 

## Step 3: Layout and Generation (AI Task)

This is the phase that traditionally takes the longest: turning text into well-designed slides. 

Instead of moving each text box manually, generate the slide layout and review the result against your source.

## Step 4: Human Review and Polish (Human Task)

Never publish AI-generated content blindly. You must act as the final art director and editor.

*   **Check the Hook:** Is the text on slide 1 massive and compelling? 
*   **Check the Flow:** Does each slide naturally lead into the next? 
*   **Check the CTA:** Is the final slide directing the user to take a specific action (e.g., "Join the newsletter" or "Leave a comment")?

If a slide feels too text-heavy, edit it down. 

## Step 5: Export and Publish

Different platforms require different file formats. 

*   **LinkedIn:** Export your carousel as a single PDF document. [LinkedIn also accepts PPT, PPTX, DOC, and DOCX](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a519831). PDF is the choice for this workflow; the format does not establish a distribution benefit.
*   **Instagram:** Export your carousel as a series of high-resolution images (JPG or PNG) and upload them as a multi-image post.

## Practical Example: One Source Through the Workflow

Consider this fictional workshop brief: “Participants bring a laptop, install the listed software before arrival, and use the supplied practice files. The session ends with a reviewed exercise.” This is an editorial example, not a measured customer case or an actual generated output.

Give the brief to GoToFlow as the source and ask for a preparation checklist for first-time participants. A possible sequence is: cover (“Prepare for your first workshop”); equipment; software preparation; practice files; final check and the actual booking-instructions link. Five slides comes from this source, not an optimal-count rule.

Compare each proposed slide with the brief. Reject an invented download link, fee, software version, or promise that participants will master the subject in one session. Supply the real details before exporting. Open the PDF or images, check text and page order on a phone, and then inspect the destination platform's upload preview. Record your actual input, review, and export time if you want to evaluate efficiency; the example itself supplies no timing benchmark.

## Conclusion

The AI carousel workflow is a collaboration. You provide the unique insight, strategy, source material, and final approval. The AI handles source analysis, structure, slide copy, visual direction, layout, and formatting. By adopting this workflow, you can drastically reduce your production time, allowing you to publish high-value carousels consistently without the burnout of manual design.
