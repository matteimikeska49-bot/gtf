# How to Write Carousel Copy with AI: Hooks, Slide Flow, and CTA Examples

Writing copy for a carousel is completely different from drafting a standard social media caption. It requires pacing, structure, and a deep understanding of how users consume content on platforms like LinkedIn and Instagram.

If your text is too dense, readers will scroll past. If your first slide lacks a hook, they won’t even start swiping. 

## What carousel copy is (and why it's different)

Carousel copy is text designed specifically for a multi-slide format. While a caption is consumed linearly, a carousel requires active participation. Every swipe is a decision.

:::cards
type: takeaways

### 1. Pacing is everything
You can’t dump a paragraph onto a single slide. Carousel copy must be broken down into bite-sized, scannable thoughts.

### 2. High signal, low noise
Fluff kills carousels. Every sentence must deliver value or push the narrative forward.

### 3. Visual relationship
The copy doesn’t live in a vacuum. It must balance with the negative space and visual elements of the slide.
:::

## Slide-by-slide structure

A high-converting carousel follows a proven narrative arc. For founders, creators, and B2B marketers, this structure works consistently:

1. **Slide 1 (The Hook):** Grab attention immediately. State the problem or the promise.
2. **Slide 2 (The Agitation):** Why does this problem matter? Make the reader relate.
3. **Slides 3–7 (The Solution):** Step-by-step frameworks, tactics, or insights. One thought per slide.
4. **Slide 8 (The Summary):** A quick recap or key takeaway.
5. **Slide 9 (The CTA):** Tell them exactly what to do next.

## Hooks: Earning the swipe

The hook is the most critical part of your carousel. If Slide 1 fails, the rest of your hard work is wasted. 

### Strong hook formats:

* **The Contrarian:** "Why everything you know about X is wrong."
* **The Blueprint:** "The exact framework we used to achieve Y."
* **The Mistake:** "7 mistakes ruining your Z (and how to fix them)."

> [!tip]
> Keep your hook under 10 words if possible. Large, bold typography on the cover slide dramatically increases the tap-through rate.

## Pacing and readability

The biggest mistake creators make is treating a carousel slide like a page in a book. 

:::cards
type: workflow

### 1. The 30-word rule
Aim for a maximum of 20-30 words per slide. If you exceed this, the font size shrinks, and mobile readability plummets.

### 2. Bullet points are your friend
If you are listing items, use bullet points. They are much easier to scan than comma-separated sentences.

### 3. Let it breathe
Don't fear negative space. Fewer words on a slide often make the message feel more authoritative and impactful.
:::

## CTA examples for carousels

You’ve guided the reader through your content. Now, you need a Call to Action (CTA) that converts.

1. **For Leads:** "Want the full template? Click the link in my bio."
2. **For Engagement (LinkedIn):** "What’s your take on this strategy? Drop a comment below."
3. **For Reach (Instagram):** "Save this post for your next content planning session."

## Common mistakes to avoid

:::cards
type: mistakes

### 1. The text wall
Cramming 100 words onto a single slide. Nobody will read it.

### 2. The weak ending
Ending the carousel abruptly without a summary or a clear CTA. You lose the conversion.

### 3. Inconsistent flow
Jumping between complex ideas without proper transitions between slides.
